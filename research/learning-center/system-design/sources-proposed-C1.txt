# Sources proposed by the F2-C1 second reader (2026-10-15). Not added to any lesson; each would let the writer keep a clause that its current cite does not support. All fetched 2026-10-15 with a scripted GET, HTTP 200.

sd-networking
  https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1034.html
    kind: doc (RFC 1034, Domain Names, Concepts and Facilities, November 1987), section 4.1.4 or thereabouts
    supports: "the realities of Internet performance suggest that these times should be on the order of days for the typical host. If a change can be anticipated, the TTL can be reduced prior to the change to minimize inconsistency during the change, and then increased back to its former value following the change."
    why: dns-and-ttl, second paragraph, attributes this to "the classic DNS specification" under a cite to SRE book ch. 19, which does not say it.
  https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8446.html
    kind: doc (RFC 8446, TLS 1.3, August 2018), section 2.3 "0-RTT Data" and appendix E.5
    supports: early data "is not forward secret" and "there are no guarantees of non-replay between connections"
    why: tls-quic-and-termination says "the same caveat applies to TLS 1.3 early data over TCP" under cites to HPBN ch. 4 and RFC 9000, neither of which says it.

sd-storage-engines (optional; the lesson is accepted without it)
  https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/wiki/Universal-Compaction
    kind: repo (RocksDB wiki)
    supports: "Double Size Issue: In universal style compaction, sometimes full compaction is needed. In this case, output data size is similar to input size."
    why: the leveled-versus-tiered table says tiered is "briefly about double during a merge of the largest run"; the cited Compaction page says only that transient space amplification "is large" in that case.
